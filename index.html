University of Oldenburg, Germany  ·  Faculty V, School of Mathematics and Science

I work at the intersection of quantum computing and artificial intelligence, designing the next generation of generative models and language understanding systems grounded in quantum mechanical principles. My research spans quantum transformer architectures, hybrid classical–quantum pipelines, and multimodal quantum generation — evaluated on NISQ-era.

A distinctive thread of my work is Quantum NLP for African low-resource languages: applying quantum information theory to develop small-data-friendly models for quantum NLP an area where conventional AI still need improvements

Themes & Projects

Research

⚛️
Quantum Natural Language Processing
Quantum-native compositional semantics, context-sensitive word embeddings, and scalable QNLP architectures. Particular focus on African low-resource languages — where data-efficient quantum methods could have real impact (DAAD-funded).
🔄
Quantum Generative Modeling
Quantum encoder–decoder architectures with quantum attention mechanisms, evaluated on Qiskit and Pennylane simulators. Targeting multimodal generation across text, audio, and image modalities.
🔗
Hybrid Classical–Quantum Pipelines
Parameterized quantum circuits (PQC) integrated with classical neural networks under NISQ-era hardware constraints. Focus on representation capacity, gradient stability, and generalization bounds.
🔬
Quantum Algorithms & Optimization
QAOA-based approaches to NP-hard problems including SAT (M.Sc. thesis), QUBO formulations, and quantum ML primitives: SVM, clustering, regression on near-term devices.

Quantum Attention Mechanism

At the core of the quantum transformer is a parameterized circuit that encodes token representations into quantum states. Entangling gates create the attention-like correlations that classical dot-product attention approximates — but within the full Hilbert space of the circuit.
